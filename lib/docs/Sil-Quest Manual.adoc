== About this game

Sil-Quest is a fork of Sil-Q, which is itself a fork of Sil. If you know Sil or
Sil-Q, much of the core dungeon-crawling experience will feel familiar: precise
tactical combat, a sophisticated stealth system, morale, smithing, song, and the
central impossible errand of stealing a Silmaril from Morgoth.

Sil-Quest keeps that foundation while adding a quest/scenario framework, web
support, mobile-oriented interface work, and cleanup of some legacy or unused
features. New authored content should generally be written as text scenarios
under `lib/edit/quests/` rather than as binary savefiles.

This manual describes the Sil-Q-derived mechanics that Sil-Quest inherits, while
updating the framing to refer to Sil-Quest as its own game. For content-authoring
details, see the `quest-authoring.adoc` document.

== About Sil

image::angband-gates.jpg[Elves before the gates of Angband,float="right",width=28%]

Sil is a role-playing game with a strong emphasis on discovery and tactical
combat. Its combat system is simple to read but rich in consequences: heavy
armour makes you easier to hit but reduces incoming damage, heavy weapons can
overcome armour, and lighter weapons can favour accuracy and critical hits.

Sil is also unusually faithful to Tolkien's First Age. It avoids many generic
fantasy conventions: there are no wizards, priests, plate mail, fireball spells,
or teleportation scrolls. Instead, warriors clad in mail sing songs of fear,
binding, rage, sorrow, and escape.

Sil belongs to the roguelike tradition: randomly generated dungeons, permanent
death, unidentified items, tactical resource pressure, and a compact symbolic
interface. Sil-Quest adds a web frontend and semantic overlays, but the core
roguelike bargain remains: choose carefully, because each character has one
life.

=== Who will like Sil?

You may like Sil-Quest if you want a detailed, well-balanced tactical
role-playing game; if you enjoy stealth and positioning as much as raw damage;
or if you want a Tolkien-inspired game that feels less generic than most
fantasy RPGs.

== The Basics

You take the role of an elf, dwarf, or human attempting the impossible: infiltrate
Morgoth's fortress of Angband, descend one thousand feet into the cold earth,
find Morgoth in his throne room, cut a Silmaril from his crown, and escape.

After character creation you begin just inside Angband with limited supplies.
Your first task is to find a weapon, then secure equipment, develop skills, and
learn which fights to seek, avoid, or end before they truly begin.

The best way to learn the basics is to play the tutorial. In game, press `?` to
open help.

Dice notation uses `XdY`, meaning roll `X` dice with `Y` sides and add the
results. For example, `2d6` means two six-sided dice.

== Notes for Angband players

Sil borrows much of Angband's interface lineage, but most mechanics are changed.

----

      │                      └─> ...
Rogue ┤
      │                      ┌─> ...
      └─> Moria ──> Angband ─┼─> Sil ──> Sil-Q ──> Sil-Quest
                             └─> ...
----


Main differences from Angband-style expectations include:

* The goal is not simply to kill Morgoth.
* The aim is to free a Silmaril from Morgoth's crown, then escape.
* The game is much shorter.
* Morgoth is found at 1,000 ft.
* There is no town or word of recall.
* There are no classes or experience levels.
* Character development is based on 8 skills and their ability trees.
* Combat, stealth, morale, and monster AI are much more tactical.
* Magic is subtle and thematic rather than spell-list driven.
* There is an effective time limit: over time, you can no longer find your way
  back to shallower depths.

System differences include:

* Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, and Grace replace the usual Angband stats.
* Hit points do not rise with experience; they depend on Constitution.
* There are four main elemental attack types: fire, cold, poison, and darkness.
* Resistances stack, reducing damage to one half, then one third, then one
  quarter, and so on.
* Darkness resistance is determined by light level.
* Poison damages over time rather than as one direct hit.
* Speed is less granular and less available.
* `{special}` is the main pseudo-identification category for artefacts and
  enchanted items.
* Tunnelling requires shovels or mattocks.

== About Sil-Q and Sil-Quest

Sil has many elegant ideas: stealth that matters, morale that changes how
monsters behave, critical hits that make light and heavy weapons feel different,
and distinct skill trees for smithing, song, perception, will, combat, and
stealth.

Sil-Q refined many of Sil's weaker elements. Sil-Quest starts from that refined
base and then makes the game easier to maintain, author, and play in modern
contexts, especially the browser.

=== More choices should be viable

Sil-Q removed or reworked many item and skill choices that were too weak,
misleading, or narrow. Some weak items were removed or made useful. Some
mechanics were introduced or adjusted so previously marginal weapons and builds
could matter. Skills such as Impale and Vengeance support weapon and build
classes that previously had little reason to exist.

Sil-Quest inherits that design direction. Its own cleanup work should follow the
same principle: remove misleading options where possible, and make remaining
options serve a clear gameplay purpose.

=== Morgoth should remain terrifying

Sil famously made killing Morgoth possible, even if thematically absurd. Sil-Q
made Morgoth less of a hit-point mountain but more dangerous when angered.
Sil-Quest keeps the theme that facing Morgoth should be a desperate act, not a
routine optimization puzzle.

=== Skills should matter throughout the game

Several Sil skills were mainly valued for the throne room. Sil-Q replaced or
rebalanced those so that investment in song, smithing, archery, stealth, and
other trees could pay off during the whole descent. Sil-Quest should continue to
treat skills as build-shaping tools rather than late-game tax payments.

=== A kinder start and better interfaces

Sil-Q made the early dungeon somewhat less punishing for players who had not yet
mastered stealth and morale. Sil-Quest continues this accessibility direction at
the interface layer: semantic web UI, mobile-friendly controls, contextual
buttons, improved overlays, and scenario-authored tutorial content.

== The Dungeon

[verse,role=section-quote]
____
"Far, far below the deepest delving of the Dwarves,
the world is gnawed by nameless things.”
____

Deep below Thangorodrim go the pits of Angband, and many and sprawling are the
corridors and caverns. It is so vast and lacking in order that having once left
an area behind, it is highly unlikely that you could navigate your way back.

Deep, deep within the earth, a thousand feet underground, is the throne room of
the black enemy Morgoth. There he holds court with all manner of foul orcs, beasts,
and demons, and in his black iron crown are those three stolen gems, the Silmarils
of Fëanor. It is normally the greatest and darkest of his servants who are found
in the deep parts of the dungeons.

=== Staircases and minimum depth

Staircases connect dungeon levels, but Sil's descent is not a leisurely shopping
trip. They normally take you 50 feet deeper or shallower, while shafts dive 100
feet into the rock. But the dark power of Morgoth calls all new things in
Angband toward him, and you can resist that pull for only so long.

As time passes, the game raises your minimum depth. If you try to return above
that depth before claiming a Silmaril, the staircase collapses and you fall back
down instead. Your character sheet records this as `Min Depth`.

Only with a Silmaril in hand can you return all the way to the surface. The
purpose of this rule is to stop slow accumulation of safety and power in the
shallows: if you want the strength to face the throne room in time, you must
press down toward the greater risks and rewards of the deep dungeon.

If you use many staircases in a short period, you may become lost and fail to
arrive where expected. Monsters can also enter levels on staircases, making them
dangerous places to linger while recovering from a fight.

=== Items and knowledge

Items often begin unidentified. Learning what objects do is part of play:
potions, herbs, staves, horns, jewellery, enchanted equipment, artefacts, and
curses all have knowledge states.

The most common way to identify an item is to cause it to have a visible effect.
For example, drinking an unidentified Potion of Antidote while poisoned will
identify that potion type for the rest of the game. Drinking the same potion
while not poisoned teaches you nothing. Potions of Healing are a special case:
they are identified the first time they are quaffed, even if no health is
restored.

Knowledge-focused abilities can reveal item properties earlier. If you can make
an item through smithing, you can identify that kind of item in the dungeon:
Jeweller identifies jewellery, Alchemy identifies herbs, potions, staves, and
horns, and Channeling identifies staves and horns. Staves of Understanding and
Self Knowledge can also help. You still gain identification experience only when
you actually encounter or identify new item types during play.

== Race and House

image::elves.jpg[Elven warriors illustration,float="right",width=30%]

Race and house define your starting identity and shape your stats, affinities,
masteries, penalties, and weapon proficiencies.

The races are intentionally not balanced against one another. The Noldor are the
most forgiving and are a good first choice. The Naugrim and Sindar are somewhat
harder but encourage interesting variations in play. The Edain are noticeably
more difficult and should be treated as a challenge mode. Within a race, the
houses are intended to be roughly comparable in power.

Each race and house modifies Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, and Grace. It
may also grant affinities, masteries, penalties, or weapon proficiencies:

`Affinity`::
  Gives +1 to the skill total. Abilities in that tree cost 500 experience less,
  and the first ability in the tree is free.

`Mastery`::
  Acts like a double affinity for one skill: +2 to the skill total, abilities
  cost 1000 experience less, and the first ability is free.

`Penalty`::
  Gives -1 to the skill total and makes abilities in that tree cost 500
  experience more.

`Weapon proficiency`::
  Gives +1 with a weapon family while the appropriate weapon is used. Elves have
  bow proficiency; dwarves have axe proficiency.

Elves inherit bow proficiency and song affinity rather than blade proficiency and
bow affinity. This helps balance archery and makes songs more available.

== Stats

Sil-Quest inherits the Sil-Q stat model. Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, and
Grace remain the four primary stats. In the main game you have 13 points to
spend between them at character creation. Costs follow the triangular numbers:
1, 3, 6, 10.

`Strength`::
  Increases melee damage, though this bonus is limited by weapon weight. It also
  increases bow range, carrying capacity, throwing range, heavy weapon handling,
  resistance to disarming, escaping webs, and bashing down locked doors.

`Dexterity`::
  Gives a bonus to the first four skills: Melee, Archery, Evasion, and Stealth.
  It also helps avoid needle traps in chests.

`Constitution`::
  Determines health. Each point increases health by 20%.

`Grace`::
  Gives a bonus to the last four skills: Perception, Will, Smithing, and Song.
  Each point increases Voice by 20%.

== Skills

Skills are the main form of character development. You spend experience to raise
skill values, then buy abilities from the associated ability trees.

The main mechanic in Sil-Quest is the skill check. For most checks, you roll
`1d10 + skill` and compare it against `1d10 + difficulty`; your total must be
higher to succeed. For Melee, Archery, and Evasion, the game uses `1d20` instead
of `1d10`, making combat swingier and allowing dramatic critical hits.

Skill checks are often opposed by an enemy skill. Melee attacks oppose enemy
Evasion, stealth opposes enemy Perception, and many songs or status effects
oppose enemy Will. Your skills can be viewed and improved from the character
menu.

=== Stealth

Stealth helps you avoid being noticed. It is opposed by monster Perception and
is modified by distance, doors, noise, armour weight, combat, songs, and recent
actions. Stealth mode gives +5 Stealth but lowers speed by 1.

Compared to original Sil, early creatures tend to have lower perception, while
deeper creatures tend to have higher perception and will. This represents
Morgoth's creatures becoming more aware of the player as the descent continues.

=== Perception

Perception helps you notice traps, secret doors, and invisible opponents. It
also helps disarm traps and pick locks.

Trap and secret-door detection uses a base of half the dungeon level, plus 10
for floor traps and secret doors, 15 for chest traps, 5 if you cannot see
properly, 5 if confused, and 2 for each square of distance beyond the first.
Disarming and lock-picking use the trap or lock's own difficulty, with extra
penalties for locks, poor sight, and confusion. If you fail to disarm a trap by
3 or more, you trigger it.

=== Will

Will helps resist many special effects: confusion, stunning, fear, blindness,
entrancement, slowing, hallucination, hunger, and stat draining. If an enemy
caused the effect, the difficulty is often based on that enemy's Will; otherwise
the default difficulty is usually 10.

Will also helps you use staves and horns, and increases the effectiveness of
many of them.

=== Smithing

Smithing was substantially reworked in Sil-Q and remains central in Sil-Quest.
Guaranteed forges exist at important depths, and armour and weapons can have
their basic stats modified without needing the old Artistry skill. Protection is
cheaper, evasion and accuracy are more expensive, and high-tier effects carry
higher costs.

=== Melee

Melee governs close combat accuracy and many physical combat abilities.

=== Archery

Archery governs bow attacks and ranged ability investment.

=== Evasion

Evasion is the primary defensive skill against physical attacks.

=== Song

Song powers the subtle magic of Sil-Quest: fear, revelation, silence, healing,
warding, protection, mastery, and layered themes.

== Combat

image::fallen-warrior.jpg[Fallen warrior illustration,float="right",width=28%]

Each attack has two stages: first the game checks whether the blow hits, then it
checks how much damage gets through.

Weapons and armour display compact combat notation:

* Offense comes first and is written in round brackets: `Battle Axe (-3,3d4)`.
  This axe gives -3 accuracy and rolls `3d4` damage when it hits.
* Defence comes second and is written in square brackets: `Leather Armour
  [-1,1d4]`. This armour gives -1 Evasion and absorbs `1d4` damage.
* Some items have both offensive and defensive modifiers. For example,
  `Greatsword (-2,3d5) [+1]` gives an attack penalty, a damage roll, and an
  Evasion bonus from parrying.
* Armour can also penalize offense: `Mail Corslet (-1) [-3,2d4]` gives -1 to
  attacks, -3 to Evasion, and `2d4` protection.

Some weapon stats differ from original Sil, especially where old weapons were
weaker than starting equipment.

=== Attacking

To see whether a melee attack hits, the attacker rolls:

[source]
----
attacker's Melee + 1d20
----

The defender rolls:

[source]
----
defender's Evasion + 1d20
----

If the attack roll is greater, the attack hits. Melee and Evasion scores include
weapon and armour modifiers, status effects such as stunning, and relevant
abilities.

Being surrounded is extremely dangerous. Overwhelming affects only Evasion:
enemies on the far side of the attacker contribute larger penalties than enemies
near the attacker, and even weak monsters can become deadly once they surround
you. Monsters never suffer this penalty, since there is only one of you.

Visibility also matters. If an attacker cannot see the defender, the attacker's
Melee is halved. If the defender cannot see the attacker, Evasion is halved.
Unwary enemies count as unable to see you for this purpose. Fighting from a pit
or web halves both Melee and Evasion again. A creature unable to move, such as a
sleeping enemy or an entranced player, has Evasion set to -5 regardless of other
modifiers.

=== Damage

If an attack hits, the attacker rolls damage and the defender rolls protection.
If damage is less than or equal to protection, no damage is dealt. Otherwise the
target takes one point of damage for each point by which damage exceeds
protection.

Strength adds sides to weapon dice, limited by weapon weight. A character with
Strength 3 using a `3d4` battle axe would normally roll `3d7`; if the axe weighs
only 4 lb, no more than four Strength-derived sides can be added. Negative
Strength subtracts sides under the same limit.

Hand-and-a-half weapons can be used one-handed or two-handed. If you are not
using a shield, the game assumes you are wielding such a weapon two-handed and
adds two damage sides.

Extra damage dice come mainly from slays, brands, and critical hits. A slaying
weapon adds a die against the relevant enemy. Elemental brands add a die against
enemies that do not resist the element, and two dice against enemies that are
explicitly vulnerable to it.

Critical hits reward high accuracy and lighter weapons. The first critical die
requires beating the Evasion roll by:

[source]
----
7 + weapon weight
----

Each additional multiple of that amount adds another die. A 3 lb longsword
therefore needs to beat Evasion by 10 for the first bonus die, 20 for the
second, 30 for the third, and so on.

=== Archery

Archery is similar to melee, but uses the Archery skill, adds the arrow's attack
bonus, and suffers -1 for every 5 squares of range. The defender's Evasion is
halved against archery, which can stack with other halvings.

Bow damage uses Strength, slays, brands, and critical hits much like melee.
Strength is limited by bow weight. Bow range is 1.5 times the number of damage
sides; a longbow dealing `1d10` has range 15.

Firing while enemies are adjacent is dangerous. Unless an ability says
otherwise, adjacent enemies get free attacks against you at half your normal
Evasion.

=== Thrown weapons

Throwing is also melee-like, but many objects are not designed to be thrown.
Daggers, throwing axes, and spears are suitable throwing weapons; unsuitable
objects suffer -5 to hit. All throws suffer -1 attack per 5 squares.

Damage sides are halved if the object was not designed for throwing. Range is
based on the object's weight and your carrying capacity:

[source]
----
carrying capacity / (5 * (weight + 2))
----

Thrown weapons display their range when examined.

=== Consumables, horns, and special items

Consumables include herbs, potions, and staves. Horns are also found in Angband;
unlike staves, horns do not run out of charges, but cost Voice when blown.

Each run randomizes the visible descriptions of unidentified consumables. A
healing potion that was golden in one game may be purple in the next, but once a
description is identified it remains consistent for that run.

Special items include ego items and artefacts. Slaying weapons and other special
equipment are identified by seeing their effects. Artefacts are generated only
once, when a floor is created; if you leave that floor without finding or
identifying one, that artefact will not be generated again in that run.

== User Interface

image::combat-ui.png[Combat UI explanation from the Sil-Q manual,align="center",width=50%]

The traditional interface presents compact combat, archery, evasion/protection,
monster health, and monster morale information. Sil-Quest's web frontend
re-renders much of this information semantically, but the underlying concepts
are the same.

In native terminal-style frontends, additional windows can show information such
as combat rolls, inventory, and messages. In the web frontend, these become
panels, overlays, and contextual controls.

=== Save files

Sil-Quest supports ordinary save/load for the main game. Traditional Sil options
are tied to a character name, so a new character name starts with its own option
state. Some players therefore reuse names across attempts.

Some quests, especially tutorial or challenge-style scenario content, may be
marked `NO_SAVE` and should not be persisted in deployment builds.

== Elements and Resistances

Elemental attacks include fire, cold, poison, and darkness.

`Fire`::
  The most common and dangerous element. It can destroy arrows, bows, axes,
  polearms, hafted weapons, boots, gloves, cloaks, soft armour, chests, torches,
  and staves.

`Cold`::
  Less common than fire. It can destroy potions and flasks of oil.

`Poison`::
  Damages over time rather than as one immediate burst. Poison damage is added
  to a poison counter; each round, about 20% of the remaining counter is dealt
  as damage and removed from the counter.

`Darkness`::
  Is resisted through light. There is no ordinary darkness resistance; instead,
  light level above 2 grants one level of darkness resistance for each excess
  point.

Resistances stack: one source reduces damage to one half, two to one third,
three to one quarter, and so on.

Elemental attacks can be pure, like dragon breath and poison gas, or mixed with
physical damage, like a balrog's flaming sword. Mixed elemental damage adds an
extra damage die if you lack resistance, or two extra dice if you are especially
vulnerable. Multiple sources of resistance do not further reduce mixed
elemental damage.

Breath attacks spread in a cone and lose `2d4` damage per square travelled.
Armour usually does not protect against them; only special protection from
abilities, magical rings and amulets, and sometimes shields against fire or cold,
are counted before resistance divides the remaining damage.

== Morale

Sil-Quest inherits Sil-Q's correction to Majesty: Majesty lowers morale by half
the difference between your Will and the monster's Will, not by -1 per 4 points
of player Will.

Monsters are not just bags of hit points. Their morale changes as they are
wounded, see allies die, notice you, hear songs, or face a character of great
majesty. A monster may be aggressive, confident, wavering, or fleeing. Learning
to make enemies flee, or to keep them from becoming confident, is part of the
tactical game.

Most enemies evaluate the situation instead of always fighting to the death.
Mindless creatures are the main exception. Morale begins with a base value and
then rises or falls with circumstances.

Morale bonuses include:

* the base morale value;
* appearing later than the monster's usual depth;
* the player escaping Angband with a Silmaril;
* the player being blind, hallucinating, stunned, confused, slow, afraid,
  entranced, or knocked out;
* the player being wounded, especially below half or quarter health;
* the monster being hasted;
* similar nearby creatures that are not fleeing, especially leaders.

Morale penalties include:

* appearing earlier than the monster's usual depth;
* the monster being stunned or badly wounded;
* light-susceptible monsters standing in strong light;
* Majesty, based on the difference between your Will and the monster's Will;
* Bane, against the selected enemy category;
* similar nearby creatures fleeing, especially leaders.

Temporary morale modifiers decay by about 10% each turn. They include being
unable to escape, just starting or stopping flight, seeing similar creatures
killed, being struck by slays or especially harmful brands, suffering Cruel
Blow, hearing Song of Elbereth, or being affected by Horns of Terror and Staves
of Majesty.

The resulting morale determines stance:

`Aggressive`::
  High morale. The monster attacks directly. Mindless creatures, trolls, angered
  enemies, and creatures affected by wrath use Aggressive behaviour instead of
  ordinary confidence.

`Confident`::
  Positive morale. The monster may use tactics while engaging you, such as
  waiting in rooms or choosing favourable approaches.

`Fleeing`::
  Morale of 0 or less. The monster tries to get away. Creatures immune to
  non-magical fear stay confident instead of fleeing.

== Stealth

In the tale behind Sil, Beren and Luthien enter Angband through secrecy and song,
not by cutting down every guard. Sil-Quest keeps stealth as a first-class way to
play.

Monster alertness broadly moves through these states:

`Asleep`::
  The monster is unaware and vulnerable.

`Unwary`::
  The monster is awake but has not fully noticed you.

`Alert`::
  The monster has noticed you and can act against you normally.

Internally, monster awareness is tracked as an Alertness score. An Alertness of
0 or more means the monster is aware of you. From -1 to -10 it is awake but
unwary. At -11 or below it is usually asleep, though not every creature in
Angband sleeps.

Each round, you roll:

[source]
----
your Stealth + 1d10
----

Each monster rolls:

[source]
----
monster Perception + 1d10
----

If the monster's total is higher, its Alertness rises by the difference. This
can wake sleeping enemies, make unwary enemies alert, or draw hostile attention.

Helpful stealth factors include:

* distance from the monster;
* closed doors between you and the monster;
* Stealth mode, which gives +5 Stealth but lowers speed;
* passing a turn, which gives a larger temporary quietness bonus that does not
  stack with Stealth mode.

Hindrances include:

* armour weight;
* attacking or being attacked;
* unwary monsters seeing you in line of sight;
* being in open spaces with many adjacent passable squares;
* singing, based on the song's noise value;
* setting off traps;
* landing from a leap.

Your light level does not directly penalize Stealth. Stealth is abstract: it can
mean moving quietly, keeping to shadow, making noises that blend into the
dungeon, or simply not being where enemies expect you to be.

If you attack a monster, or an unaware monster tries to move into your square, it
notices you immediately. Once a monster has noticed you, it can become unwary
again only if you are out of sight and it fails its perception check badly
enough. This normally requires beating it by more than 25; Vanish reduces this
threshold to 15. Songs, staves, and territorial behaviour can also make monsters
unwary or asleep.

Compared to original Sil, Sil-Q and Sil-Quest make early monsters less
perceptive and deeper monsters more perceptive and willful.

== Smithing

Angband contains forges used by Morgoth's hosts, and a skilled character can turn
them to other purposes. Smithing requires:

* a forge with remaining uses;
* the relevant Smithing ability;
* the base object or category you want to make;
* sufficient Smithing skill for the planned difficulty;
* any required experience, stat, or special costs.

Making an item takes ten turns per point of difficulty and makes noise, so you
may be interrupted. Interrupted forging can be resumed later at the same forge.
You can use smithing planning mode at a forge to explore possibilities before
committing resources.

Some equipment slots are easier to enchant than others. Weapons, bows, shields,
body armour, helms, and amulets have lower costs than most other slots, and
robes are cheaper still. In the main dungeon, there are guaranteed forges at
100 ft, 300 ft, and 500 ft, but only the first time those depths are generated.
If a shaft skips over such a depth, the guaranteed forge appears at the depth
where you land instead.

Sil-Quest inherits Sil-Q's guaranteed important forges and its smithing cost
rebalance. Protection is cheaper, Evasion and accuracy are more expensive, and
high-tier effects cost more experience. Authored scenarios can also place forges
directly in map terrain.

=== Creating artefacts

With Artifice, truly great smiths can make custom artefacts: beautiful or
terrible works that may become the matter of songs and legends. Artefacts can
combine properties beyond the usual limits, can be named by the player, and are
almost indestructible. Creating one is a great work and requires three uses from
the same forge.

=== Enchanted forges

Most forges arm Morgoth's hosts with swords and mail, but a few are greater
works used for the axes of Valaraukar or the armour of mighty trolls. Enchanted
forges give a Smithing bonus, allowing you to create items beyond your ordinary
reach.

== Temporary Status Effects

At certain times you may suffer temporary effects. Many appear in coloured text
near the bottom of the map or in the web status area. Most wear off with time,
and many have some consumable or ability that can remove them immediately.

=== Hunger

The Gorged status no longer exists. Characters may still eat and drink when
maximally full, but after the message that they are as full as possible,
additional food is digested extremely rapidly.

Hunger states are:

`Full`::
  At least 5,000 turns of food.

`Hungry`::
  Below the comfortable range, but not yet dangerous.

`Weak`::
  Around 1,000 turns of food or less. You suffer -1 Strength until you eat
  enough to recover.

`Starving`::
  No food remains. You take damage each turn and no longer regenerate health.

=== Negative effects

`Blind`::
  You cannot see. Melee, Evasion, and Archery are halved against foes you cannot
  see.

`Hallucinating`::
  Enemies and objects appear as other things, and you may occasionally see
  things that are not there.

`Slow`::
  You move at half speed. If this is caused by excessive carried weight, you
  must reduce your load to remove it.

`Confused`::
  You cannot reliably choose directions. Directional actions may go somewhere
  nearby instead.

`Afraid`::
  You are too afraid to attack by melee, archery, or throwing.

`Entranced`::
  You cannot act and have -5 Evasion. It can be broken by an enemy attack, time,
  or Freedom of Movement.

`Stunned`::
  Stunned characters suffer -2 to all skills. Heavy stunning worsens this to
  -4. Extreme stunning can knock the character out, leaving them unable to act
  with -5 Evasion.

`Poisoned X`::
  You will take `X` poison damage over the next few rounds. Each round, about
  20% of the remaining amount is dealt and removed.

`Bleeding X`::
  An open wound, handled similarly to poison.

=== Drained stats and resistances

Drained Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, or Grace appears in yellow. Stat
drain does not heal with time; you need an appropriate item to restore it.

Elemental resistances are covered in the elements section. Resistance to a
status effect grants a large bonus, usually +10, to rolls against that effect.
Free Action resists slowness and entrancement and helps escape webs. See
Invisible grants +10 to seeing otherwise invisible enemies.

=== Regeneration

Regeneration increases hunger only while you are actually regenerating. Voice
recovery is also affected by regeneration. In practice, regeneration heals you
at three times the normal rate and improves Voice recovery, but makes you hungry
much faster while it is active.

== Experience

Sil has neither classes nor experience levels. Experience is spent directly on
skills and abilities.

Common sources of experience include:

* encountering new monster types;
* killing monsters;
* descending deeper;
* identifying new item types;
* some scenario or quest-specific rewards.

In the ordinary main game, characters begin with 5,000 experience to allocate.
Sil-Q introduced a Fixed XP challenge with 50,000 starting experience and no
further experience gain. Sil-Quest has moved toward quest/scenario-backed starts
instead; scenario-authored quests can set starting experience explicitly with
`P:EXP` and `P:NEW_EXP`.

In ordinary main-game play, experience follows the Sil-Q model.

Encountering a monster for the first time gives experience based on the depth at
which that monster is normally found. Later encounters with the same monster
type decay: the second gives half, the third gives a third, the fourth gives a
quarter, and so on. Killing monsters gives a separate pool of experience using
the same decay. There is as much experience available from encounters as from
kills, so stealthy characters can progress without needing to murder every room
into silence.

The first time you reach a depth of `d` feet or deeper, you gain `d` experience.
Identifying a new potion, herb, ring, amulet, horn, stave, or special item gives
100 experience; identifying an artefact gives 200.

You spend experience to improve the eight skills. Your first point in a skill
costs 100 experience, your second costs 200, your third costs 300, and so on.
Abilities linked to a skill become more expensive as you buy more of them: the
first costs 500, the second 1000, the third 1500, and so forth, modified by
affinities, masteries, and penalties.

== Abilities

This section is a reference for the Sil-Q-derived ability set inherited by
Sil-Quest.

=== Melee Abilities

==== Power

* Gives +1 damage side to melee attacks.
* Increases the amount needed to score each level of critical hit by 1.
** For example, you need to hit by `8 + weapon weight` instead of
   `7 + weapon weight`.

==== Finesse

* Lowers the amount needed to score each level of critical hit by 2.
** For example, you need to hit by `5 + weapon weight` instead of
   `7 + weapon weight`.

This was strengthened from Sil, where it reduced the amount needed by 1.

==== Knock Back

* When you hit a monster, you make a skill check to see whether you force it
  back one square.
* You roll twice your effective Strength with that attack against twice the
  monster's Constitution.
* Effective Strength includes ability modifiers such as Charge or Rapid Attack,
  is capped by weapon weight, and gets +2 if wielding a weapon with two hands.
* The monster is knocked back only if an empty square is available.
* A monster actually moved by Knock Back misses its next turn.
* Opportunist attacks do not trigger Knock Back.

==== Polearm Mastery

* Gives +2 Melee when using polearms.
* Allows you to set a polearm to intercept attackers.
** If you waited last turn and an opponent who was not adjacent moves adjacent,
   you get a free attack.
** You do not get this free attack if you cannot see the opponent or are
   confused.

==== Charge

* If you attack after just moving toward an opponent, your attack is calculated
  as if you had +3 Strength and +3 Dexterity.
* The damage bonus applies only to regular, flanking, or controlled-retreat
  attacks.

==== Follow-Through

* If you kill an opponent, you get a free follow-through attack on another
  adjacent opponent.
* You do not get the free attack if you cannot see the opponent or are confused.
* The follow-up applies only after a regular, flanking, controlled-retreat, or
  follow-through attack, so it can chain.

==== Impale

* Allows you to attack up to two enemies in a row while wielding a polearm or
  greatsword.
* Both enemies are attacked with an impale attack.
* Applies only if the attack was regular, flanking, or controlled retreat.
* Rapid Attack and Smite can trigger on Impale attacks.

==== Subtlety

* If you wield a one-handed weapon and have nothing in your off-hand, the amount
  needed for each critical-hit level is reduced by 2.
* Hand-and-a-half weapons, such as Bastard Swords, do not count as one-handed.

This stacks with Finesse.

==== Whirlwind Attack

* When you attack an enemy, you get a free attack on all other adjacent enemies.
* You do get these free attacks even if you cannot see the opponent or are
  confused.
* Extra attacks are gained only on regular, flanking, or controlled-retreat
  attacks.
* All the attacks are treated as whirlwind attacks, so they are incompatible
  with many other abilities.

==== Zone of Control

* If an opponent moves between two squares that are both adjacent to you, you get
  a free attack.
* You do not get this free attack if you cannot see the opponent or are confused.

==== Smite

* When using a two-handed weapon, the first main attack you make each turn rolls
  maximum possible damage against all opponents it hits.
* If you make such an attack, you lose a turn recovering.
* Whirlwind Attack and Impale count as main attacks that can hit multiple
  enemies.

==== Two Weapon Fighting

* You can wield a one-handed weapon in your off-hand instead of a shield.
* You receive an off-hand attack calculated as if you had -3 Strength and
  -3 Dexterity.
* The extra attack applies only to regular, flanking, whirlwind, or
  controlled-retreat attacks.
* If Impale and Two Weapon Fighting would both be valid, you impale instead of
  striking with both weapons.

==== Rapid Attack

* You receive two melee attacks instead of one.
* They are calculated as if you had -3 Strength and -3 Dexterity.
* The extra attack applies only to regular, flanking, or controlled-retreat
  attacks.
* In other cases, the single attack does not suffer those penalties.

==== Strength

* You gain a point of Strength.

=== Archery Abilities

==== Rout

* Attacks on fleeing enemies are calculated as if you had +5 Dexterity.

==== Fletchery

* You can craft arrows into +3 arrows with the `-` command.
* Each +3 arrow takes one turn to craft.
* Crafting a whole stack takes as many turns as there are arrows.
* You can be interrupted while crafting.

==== Point Blank Archery

* When firing provokes attacks of opportunity, the enemy you fired at does not
  get one.

==== Puncture

* Whenever an enemy's armour roll would fully block your archery damage roll,
  you deal a flat 5 damage instead.

==== Ambush

* Grants an additional critical damage die when you hit an unwary or sleeping
  monster with an arrow.

==== Versatility

* If Archery is higher than Melee, you gain a Melee bonus equal to half the
  difference, rounded down.

==== Crippling Shot

* If you score a critical hit with archery and deal at least 1 net damage, the
  target makes a Will check against:
+
[source]
----
30 - (60 / (critical level + 2))
----
* On failure, the target is slowed for one round per critical level.

==== Deadly Hail

* When shooting an enemy immediately after killing another enemy with an arrow,
  you roll twice as many damage dice as normal.
* All damage dice are doubled: slay, poison, critical, and weapon dice all
  count.
* The target only rolls armour once.

==== Dexterity

* You gain a point of Dexterity.

=== Evasion Abilities

==== Dodging

* Gives +3 Evasion if you moved last turn.
* This bonus is not shown in the side panel, but it applies.

==== Blocking

* Doubles the number of protection dice granted by your shield against all
  attacks if you did not move on your last turn.

==== Parry

* Doubles the evasion bonus granted by your weapon.
* This does not apply to an off-hand weapon.

==== Crowd Fighting

* Halves the Melee bonus opponents get for surrounding you.

==== Leaping

* Lets you leap over one square of chasm or trap.
* Roosts and webs are not leapable.
* You must have moved roughly toward the obstacle on the previous turn.
* It takes two turns to reach the destination; one is spent in the air.
* You can be attacked while in the air.

==== Sprinting

* You gain +1 speed if you move four or more squares in roughly the same
  direction.
* Each move can differ by up to 45 degrees from the previous one.
* The speed lasts until you fail to meet the condition.
* You can never exceed speed 3.

==== Flanking

* Gives a free attack if you move between two squares that are both adjacent to
  an opponent.
* You cannot get both a flanking attack and a controlled-retreat attack in the
  same turn; flanking only adds eligible opponents.
* Works on one opponent each time, preferring the targeted opponent if any.
* You do not get the free attack if you cannot see the opponent or are confused.

==== Heavy Armour Use

* Gives `[1dX]` extra protection, where `X = total armour weight / 15 lb`.

==== Riposte

* If an opponent misses you in melee by at least `10 + weapon weight`, you get a
  free attack.
* You get at most one riposte per round.
* You do not get this free attack if you cannot see the opponent or are
  confused.

==== Controlled Retreat

* Gives a free attack if you move from a square adjacent to an opponent to a
  square not adjacent to it, but only if you did not move on the previous round.
* You cannot get both a flanking attack and a controlled-retreat attack in the
  same turn; controlled retreat only adds eligible opponents.
* Works on one opponent each time, preferring the targeted opponent if any.
* You do not get the free attack if you cannot see the opponent or are confused.

==== Dexterity

* You gain a point of Dexterity.

=== Stealth Abilities

==== Disguise

* Halves bonuses that awake but unwary monsters have to notice you because you
  are in their line of sight.

==== Assassination

* Gives a Melee bonus equal to your Stealth when attacking unwary or sleeping
  opponents.
* You do not get the bonus if you cannot see the opponent or are confused.
* The bonus applies only to regular, flanking, or controlled-retreat attacks.
* The bonus is not applied to charge attacks.

==== Cruel Blow

* If you score a critical hit in melee and deal at least 1 net damage, the target
  makes a Will check against:
+
[source]
----
30 - (60 / (critical level + 2))
----
* On failure, the target becomes confused with pain for one round per critical
  level.

==== Exchange Places

* Use the `X` command to swap places with an adjacent enemy.
* Alert enemies get a free attack unless they are confused or mindless.
* Cannot be used when you cannot see the enemy, or while you are in a web or pit.

==== Opportunist

* If an opponent moves from a square adjacent to you to one that is not, you get
  a free attack.
* You do not get the free attack if you cannot see the opponent or are confused.

==== Vanish

* Makes it easier to make enemies unwary again.
* Instead of needing to beat their perception check by more than 25 while out of
  line of sight, you need to beat it by more than 15.

==== Dexterity

* You gain a point of Dexterity.

=== Perception Abilities

==== Quick Study

* Lets you take abilities without their normal prerequisite abilities.

==== Focused attack

* Gives an attack bonus equal to half your Perception if you waited on the
  previous turn.
* Works only for the first attack of the round.

==== Keen Senses

* Lets you see enemies just beyond the edge of a pool of light, including your
  own light radius.
* Gives +5 to spotting invisible enemies.

==== Concentration

* Gives +1 attack for each consecutive round spent attacking a particular enemy,
  to a maximum of half your Perception.
* Waiting or blocking does not break concentration.

==== Alchemy

* Reveals the purpose of all herbs and potions you encounter, as well as staves
  and horns.
* You still gain identification experience only when you actually encounter an
  item type you had not seen before.

==== Bane

* Gives a bonus to all skill rolls against a selected broad enemy category.
* Categories are Orc, Wolf, Spider, Troll, Wraith, Rauko, Serpent, and Dragon.
* You must have killed 4 enemies from the category to select it.
* When you reach `2n` kills of the chosen enemy, the bonus increases to `+n`.

==== Outwit

* Whenever you receive a critical hit, you make a Perception roll against the
  attacker's Perception.
* If you succeed, you negate all critical damage from that attack.

==== Listen

* Gives a chance each turn to detect monsters you cannot see, including around
  corners and through doors.
* The check is Perception versus the opponent's stealth score with a modifier, as below:
** `-3`  if awake but unwary
** `-5`  if it moved
** `-10` if it called out or smashed a door
** `-15` if it tunnelled through rock
** `+(Song / 2)` if you are singing Song of Silence
** `+1` per square of distance along the shortest sound path
** `+5` per closed door along the shortest sound path
* Success represents the opponent as a grey asterisk.
* Success by 10 or more reveals the opponent completely.

==== Master Hunter

* Gives +1 attack for each time you have killed an opponent of the same narrow
  type, up to a maximum of one half of your Perception.
* For example, killing 3 Orc soldiers gives +3 attack against Orc soldiers.

==== Grace

* You gain a point of Grace.

=== Will Abilities

==== Curse Breaking

* Lets you remove cursed equipment and thereby break the curse.

==== Channeling

* You automatically recognize all staves and horns.
* Horns have halved voice costs.
* Staves have twice as many charges.

==== Strength in Adversity

* Gives bonuses to Strength, Dexterity, and Grace when seriously injured:
** +1 at 50% health or below.
** +3 at 25% health or below.

==== Formidable

* Slaying enemies in melee scares all other enemies who see it.
* Enemies do not gain morale based on your injuries.

==== Inner Light

* Strengthens the light in your light radius against unnatural darkness created
  by some monsters.

==== Indomitable

* Gives resistance to fear, confusion, stunning, and hallucination.
* Slows hunger to one third the normal rate.

==== Oath

* You swear an oath and are rewarded for keeping it.
* If the forbidden action has already been performed before the oath is taken,
  that oath is unavailable.
* If you perform the forbidden action after taking the oath, you lose the oath's
  benefit and cannot recover it that game.
* Oaths:
** Mercy: do not attack Men or Elves; gain +1 Grace.
** Silence: do not sing; gain +1 Strength.
** Iron: do not go up stairs without a Silmaril; gain +2 Constitution.

==== Poison Resistance

* Gives a level of resistance to poison.

==== Vengeance

* When damaged in melee by an enemy, your next successful attack gains one
  additional damage die.
* This does not stack.
* A maximum of one die can be gained this way on each attack.

==== Majesty

* Makes monsters more likely to flee by lowering morale by half the difference
  between your Will and theirs.

==== Constitution

* You gain a point of Constitution.

=== Smithing Abilities

==== Weaponsmith

* Allows you to create weapons at a forge.
* Includes bows and arrows.
* Lets you improve attack, evasion, and damage values of weapons you create.

==== Armoursmith

* Allows you to create armour at a forge.
* Lets you improve attack, evasion, and protection values of armour you create.

==== Jeweller

* Allows you to create rings, amulets, light sources, and horns at a forge.
* Identifies rings, amulets, light sources, and horns you encounter.

==== Enchantment

* Allows you to create `{special}` items at a forge.
* Identifies enchanted items you encounter.

==== Expertise

* Reduces time taken to forge an item by half.
* Negates all experience and stat costs on items you create.

==== Artifice

* Allows you to create custom artefacts with many different properties.
* These require three forge uses each.

==== Masterpiece

* Allows you to create items whose difficulty exceeds your Smithing skill.
* For each excess point of difficulty, you permanently lose one Smithing point.
* You can regain lost Smithing later with more experience.

==== Grace

* You gain a point of Grace.

== Sil-Quest Web and Mobile Notes

Sil-Quest includes a web frontend in addition to the traditional terminal-style
frontends. The web frontend renders the map semantically, exposes contextual
buttons for common actions, and uses mobile-friendly overlays for character,
inventory, knowledge, help/manual, and menu views.

On phones, the side panel is normally collapsed and the bottom-right contextual
buttons are the primary touch affordance. Tapping the player's current grid can
be used to wait. Contextual actions such as singing, toggling stealth, opening
inventory, and shooting appear when they are useful.

Quest and tutorial content should be authored with this in mind:

* Keep tutorial notes and help text concise.
* Avoid very wide ASCII art unless it is essential.
* Prefer scenario note objects and semantic text blocks to terminal-only UI.
* Test new content in both a desktop-size browser and a phone-size viewport.
